What’s missing is a concrete journey from “Meteor Mite has gone quiet” to “Meteor Mite can receive suitable offers again.” Helen describes the outcome she wants, but the brief still needs buildable behavior:

- **Trigger:** What qualifies as quiet—few offers over what period, while available and eligible? Low demand, low ranking and failed notifications need different explanations.
- **Kip’s experience:** What appears on Mite’s coverage card, what can Kip learn from it, and what action can he take? Specify the text and states, including when the cause is unknown.
- **Mite’s experience:** What does Mite see or do on the phone, and what happens next? Define whether recovery is automatic or requires an action, without promising an incident will arrive.
- **Routing rules:** Exactly how eligibility or history recovers, when recovery starts and ends, and how missed pings differ from explicit turn-downs. Preserve suitable capability matching and timely assignment.
- **Acceptance criteria:** Give engineers observable examples: an available Mite with no suitable incidents gets no misleading recovery promise; a qualifying Mite receives the defined routing treatment; Kip sees its status; accepting, declining or missing the next offer produces a specified result.

The central missing decision is **what the product does to help Mite recover, and how both people understand that change**. A new number in routing plus a reassuring label would leave that decision unresolved.
